# THE SWARM — Domain Context

## Core Concepts

### Nest Capacity
The maximum number of workers the colony can support. When workers reach capacity, the `larvaPipeline` is blocked — existing workers continue normally, but no new workers hatch until capacity is expanded. This makes `nestCapacity` a real gameplay mechanic (previously cosmetic/dead code).

Capacity is expanded via:
- **Dig workers**: +1 capacity per `resourceBalance.digMsPerRoom` of one dig worker (integer-only accumulation)
- **Warehouse building**: +25 per level (soft-capped after Lv.5 via `softCapEffectiveness`)
- **"Expand" decision**: +10 one-time from DecisionSystem

Base capacity: 25. Reset to 25 on prestige.

### Workers
The ant population. Workers consume 0.5 food/sec each (linear). They can be assigned to:
- **Gather**: produce food and wood
- **Forage**: produce nectar (Story 3.4)
- **Tend**: increase egg hatch rate
- **Dig**: increase nest capacity and produce stone
- **Guard**: soldiers for combat
- **Researchers**: research projects

Unassigned (**free**) workers produce food at half gather rate.

Wood, stone and nectar come from the roles from Colony on (Story 3.4, FR36), so the first buildings are buyable before any territory; claimed territory adds to them. The yields are in `src/data/balance/roles.ts` (`roleBalance`).

**Buildings by phase** (Story 3.5, FR7): `src/data/content/buildings.ts` (`BUILDING_UNLOCK_PHASE`) puts Barracks, Walls and Warehouse on BUILD from Colony and Silk Weaving, Chitin Armory and Venom Lab from Combat (`building_panel` opens in Colony). `src/data/content/producers.ts` (`FIRST_PRODUCED_IN`) is the producer table: the earliest phase each cost resource is made in (Story 3.6 extends it). `BuildingSystem.isBuildingOpen(b, state)` = unlocked in the phase AND every resource in the next level's price has a producer by then, so the Lv.5+ combat-loot surcharge on the colony buildings waits for Combat (card shows `NOT YET`). `canBuild` and `build` include that check; `actions.buyBuilding` refuses `locked`. Selectors: `selectBuildingUnlocked(id)`, `selectBuildingOpen(id)`. **Combat's choices** (Story 4.8, FR19): the **Midden** opens in Combat too (stone only): every soldier lost in a battle, won or lost, leaves `getEffects('midden', level).salvagePerFallen` chitin (added to `BattleResult.specialLoot.chitin`, line `battle_midden`). Silk Weaving and the Chitin Armory stop at the level that opens their equipment (`BUILDING_MAX_LEVEL`, `isBuildingMaxed`; the card shows `LV.1 · MAX`, `BUILT`). The **Walls'** `defenseBonus` now multiplies the army's defence in battle (`getSoldierDefense`). `tests/sim/combatChoices.test.ts` checks that every Phase 3 purchasable gives something and that none is **strictly dominated** (`dominatesProfile` in `tests/unit/helpers/choices.ts`: another costs the same or less in every currency and gives at least as much on every measured effect); `CRITICAL_PATH[COMBAT]` lists them all.

**Catch-22 rule** (Story 3.6, FR37): `FIRST_PRODUCED_IN` covers every resource a price can name: workers and food from Egg Laying, wood/stone/nectar from Colony, combat loot and Legacy points (prestige needs Lv.5 buildings, which cost loot) from Combat, and void crystals, antimatter and dark matter from Expansion (the 10 % expedition space drops, the scout-ship bootstrap). `tests/unit/data/noCatch22.test.ts` walks every item in `NextAction.listItems()` in every phase and level it is on sale, the fleet ships, the converter's inputs and the Legacy nodes, and fails on a cost whose producer opens later; it also checks each table entry against the real producer. New purchasable content goes in `listItems()`, so the test covers it. **Particle Lab**: an item (`{ kind: 'particle_lab' }`, `actions.buildParticleLab`, `[data-particle-lab]` card above the converter, sold from Space), priced in stone and void crystals (`data/balance/conversions.ts`); the antimatter step runs from Lv.1 at one run a second per level.

**Roles and the workforce** (Story 1.23, ADR 0016): the WRKR rows come from the role data (`src/data/content/roles.ts`: Gather, Forage, Tend, Dig, Guard from Colony; Research with the research panel). A researcher leaves `resources.workers`, so the **workforce** is `workers + researchers`, the **assigned total** is the job roles plus the researchers, and **free** workers are the rest (`src/systems/Workforce.ts`). A stepper press is one `actions.stepRole(role, delta)` write; WRKR's primary action (`> MOVE 4 TO GATHER`) is `getReallocation(state)` → `actions.moveWorkers(from, to, count)`: the fewest workers that stop a food loss, or the free workers to DIG when the nest is full, otherwise none. Worker efficiency follows a diminishing-returns curve (`1/(1 + 0.0005 × (workers − 500))` for workers > 500).

### Phases
6 sequential phases: EGG_LAYING → COLONY → COMBAT → EXPANSION → SPACE → TRANSCENDENCE. Phase transitions are gated by resource/worker thresholds and battles. Each phase unlocks new panels, mechanics, and resources.

### Goal
A phase's unlock condition: its outgoing transition in `src/phases/transitions.ts`, made of **conditions** (`{ id, label, current(s), target(s) }`). The guard is derived as "every condition met", so the thresholds exist only there. The goal meter (`ui/shell/GoalMeter.tsx`) reads them through `state/selectors.ts` (`selectGoal`, `selectConditionCurrent/Target/Met`, `selectGoalPercent`) and shows `NEXT: …` without naming the next phase.

### Latch and CONTINUE
The phase never changes on its own (Story 1.19, ADR 0003). Each tick `PhaseStateMachine.evaluate` checks the goal and `latch` writes `state.phaseGoal` (`{ phase, latched, atMs, reached }`) the first time it is met. The latch survives dips and saves: the meter reads `GOAL MET`, a dipped row reads `<now> now, reached <value> at <time>` (game time), NEST gets `•` and its primary action becomes `> CONTINUE` (`[data-action="continue"]`). Tapping it calls `actions.continuePhase`, which runs `PhaseStateMachine.commit` (onEnter, clears the latch, emits `phase_committed { from, to }`). Only `commit` and prestige write `state.phase` (tests/arch/architecture.test.ts).

### Phase reveal and overlays
Committing a phase plays the **phase reveal** (Story 1.20, ADR 0014): a full-screen overlay from the one overlay layer (`ui/shell/OverlayController`, drawn by `OverlayHost`) that blocks input for at most 3.5 s, cross-fades the palette, shows the phase name and types one line. Sheets and decision events wait while an overlay is up. A reveal can be skipped (tap, Enter, Esc) only once its phase is in `state.lifetime.revealsSeen`, which survives prestige.

### Character grid and reflow

Story 1.31 (ADR 0022): text panels sit on a computed **character grid**, `cols = floor(contentWidth / advanceWidth(body))`, marked on `<html data-grid="full|medium|narrow">` (≥ 36, 28–35, < 28 columns). Below 36 meters shrink to `cols − 8` cells and two-column rows stack; below 28 text wraps, box-drawing becomes 1 px borders, meters read `74 % ████····` and aligned tables stack label over value. Nothing clips text. The primary zone un-pins below 480 px of height; NEST shows one event line below 560 px.

### Accessibility floor
Typed decoration (`·`, `→`, brackets, glyphs, 4-letter labels) is visual only: `Deco.tsx` (`Abbr`, `Sep`, `Arrow`, `decorate`) hides it and gives the words. One polite announcer (`[data-announcer]`) says goal met, phase entered, battle result, decision arrived, starving and capacity reached, on their edge, never values per tick. Reduced motion is `:root[data-reduced-motion="true"]` (`motion.css`); CRT in effect is `<html data-crt>`; `crt.css` draws it on `[data-crt-surface]` scene areas and `[data-line-work]`, OFF at boot where the device asks for contrast or large text. ADRs 0023, 0046.

### Voice
The colony speaks in short, present-tense, upper-case lines ending in a full stop; the interface speaks plainly; nothing has an exclamation mark, and no copy names a phase before it is entered (UX-DR42, Story 1.33). **Colony lines** (event lines and narrative beats) live in `src/data/content/colonyLines.ts`, each with the first phase it can be written in; `colonyLine(id, values)` fills them. Decision titles end with a full stop (`OVERCROWDING.`). Short forms (`LV.`, `STR`, `CHTN`) are registered in `src/data/content/abbreviations.ts`, and `decorate()` reads them in full. `tests/arch/voice.test.ts` scans every literal in `src/` for these rules; `tests/e2e/voice.spec.ts` reads the rendered game in every phase.

### Debug overlay, cheats and the seed
Story 1.34 (ADR 0024): `state.seed` (an unsigned 32-bit integer, save v19) is the game's seed; Story 3.1 seeds the generator from it. The **debug overlay** (`src/debug/`, loaded by main as its own chunk in dev builds or with `?debug=1`) opens with the backtick key or a 3-finger long-press: FPS, tick time p50/p95, catch-up time, seed, save version, build, a read-only state inspector, the log ring buffer and EXPORT DIAGNOSTICS. **Cheats** (`window.__swarm.cheats`: `addResources`, `setPhase`, `timeSkip`, `setSeed`) exist only in dev builds; a production build contains none of their code. E2E tests keep seeding saves and don't depend on cheats.

### Seeded randomness
Story 3.1 (ADR 0005): every random outcome of the simulation comes from the seeded generator in `src/utils/rng.ts` (mulberry32), never `Math.random` (the arch test's exception list is empty). Its state is `state.rng` (a u32, save v24), started from `rngFromSeed(state.seed)` and kept across prestige. A tick draws from `ctx.rng` (the tick context's stream, opened on `state.rng`) and folds `ctx.rng.state` into its one write; an action (a battle) opens `rngStream(state.rng)` and writes back the advanced state. Things that run over game time draw from **keyed streams** (`keyedStream(seed, prestige count, kind, id or tick)`): an expedition's or exploration's rolls are fixed by its id, a probe launch by the game tick, so outcomes never depend on step length or resolution order. The enemy card peeks the next enemy (`peekEnemy`) without drawing it. Tests pin a roll with `tests/unit/helpers/rng.ts` `fixedRng(x)`. UI-only effects may still use `Math.random`.

### Resume and lastSeenAt
Story 2.3 (ADR 0026): every way back goes through `resume(elapsedMs)` (`src/engine/resume.ts`): a cold load, the tab becoming visible, a bfcache `pageshow`, a `focus` after sleep, and a visible frame gap over 1 s. **`lastSeenAt`** (save v20) is the wall-clock time the saved state was last simulated to. Hiding the page (`visibilitychange`, `pagehide`, through the platform `Lifecycle`) saves and stops the ticker, so nothing runs while hidden. A gap under 60 s runs at full efficiency with no summary. 60 s or more is **offline time** at the offline efficiency, followed by the offline summary. Each gap is applied once, however many return signals arrive.

### Pause
Story 2.10 (FR25, ADR 0002/0026): pause is **in-session only**. The resume path owns it (`pause`/`unpause`/`isPaused`); it stops the ticker and paused time is never simulated. Hiding or closing the page ends it, and the time away is resumed like any other absence. It is never in the game state or the save (save v23 dropped `paused`). While paused the `PAUSED` overlay line is up (`[data-overlay="paused"]`); the next tap or key resumes. Entry points: the top bar's button, P, and PAUSE in settings.

### Offline cap
Story 2.4: the **offline cap** is the most resumed time that is simulated. `getOfflineCapMs(state)` (`src/systems/OfflineProgression.ts`) is the only source: `OFFLINE_CAP_BASE_MS` (12 h, `src/data/constants.ts`) plus the `offlineCapRaiseMs` of every owned Legacy upgrade (Chrono Synchronization and Long Dormancy, Story 2.8), never above `OFFLINE_CAP_MAX_MS` (24 h). `resume` (through `planOffline`: cold load, return from hidden, frame gaps) and `catchUp` (every catch-up, the dev time skip included) both cut to it; the Ticker has no frame cap; the load info and the summary sheet say whether it was reached and which value applied (`[data-offline-cap]`). `tests/arch/offlineCap.test.ts` fails on any other cap constant or time clamp.

### Offline efficiency
Story 2.8 (FR21): **offline efficiency** is computed, never stored: `getOfflineEfficiency(state)` (`src/systems/OfflineProgression.ts`) is `OFFLINE_EFFICIENCY_BASE_POINTS` (50) plus the `offlineEfficiencyPoints` of every research project this run has done (**Dormant Foraging**, sold on BUILD from Colony for food, panel `offline_research_panel`, wiped on prestige) plus those of every owned Legacy upgrade (**Temporal Resonance**), at most 100 %. The two can be bought in either order. Save v22 dropped the stored `offlineEfficiency` and granted the Legacy nodes to saves that owned the old one-off buys (0.75 → Temporal Resonance; 1.0 → also Chrono Synchronization and this run's research done).

Story 2.6: catch-up runs in **macro-steps**. `catchUp` (`src/engine/catchUp.ts`) advances the planned game time (`ticks × 50 ms`) through the same `processTick` in steps of `CATCH_UP_STEP_MS` (10 s, `src/data/constants.ts`), the last step taking what is left, and logs one `catch-up` summary line. CI checks the **step budget**: a 24 h catch-up calls `processTick` at most `CATCH_UP_MAX_STEPS` times (`tests/unit/engine/catchUpBudget.test.ts`); wall-clock time is never asserted in CI. `pnpm perf` (`vitest.perf.config.ts`, `tests/perf/`) reports the 12 h and 24 h times against `CATCH_UP_BUDGET_MS` (500 ms). It relies on Story 2.5: systems give the same result for any step length; `tests/unit/engine/catchUpEquivalence.test.ts` checks every macro-step against 50 ms steps with 2.5's tolerance (`tests/unit/helpers/stepTolerance.ts`, which now also has `stageRates`).

Story 2.7: **offline-only rules** live in `src/engine/offlineRules.ts` and `catchUp`, never at a call site. During any catch-up no decision event spawns or counts down, no phase is committed (the goal may latch), events go to a queue that is never flushed, and automation runs as the player set it. The **starvation floor** (FR24): after every catch-up step workers are kept at or above `floor(OFFLINE_WORKER_FLOOR_SHARE × workers before the absence)` (0.5, `src/data/constants.ts`); foreground play has no floor. Each rule has an invariant test in `tests/unit/engine/offlineRules.test.ts`.

Story 2.9: the **offline summary** (`src/ui/sheets/OfflineSummarySheet.tsx`) is built in main from the states before and after the resume: `YOU WERE GONE <time>. THE NEST KEPT WORKING.` (focused), the efficiency, each resource whose whole amount changed (losses in `warn` with ▼), the warn lines `UPKEEP OUTRAN FOOD FOR <time>.` and `THE NEST STOPPED GROWING AFTER <cap>.` (colony lines `offline_starved`, `offline_cap`), and one **next move**. `catchUpWithReport` (`src/engine/catchUp.ts`) measures how long food stood at zero (`starvedMs`, carried in `OfflineLoadInfo`); `catchUp` returns only its state. The next move is `getReturnMove(state)` (`src/systems/NextAction.ts`): the next-action rule's suggestion or its `NEXT AFFORDABLE` item, and only when the rule has neither, a fallback (an allocation WRKR accepts, else laying an egg), so it is never empty (FR27). Its button closes the sheet and jumps to the target's panel like a chronicle line (`[data-jumped]`). A resume under 60 s opens no sheet; `resourceFeed.flash()` flashes the strip's deltas once (`[data-resource-strip][data-flash]`).

### Saves
Story 2.2 (ADR 0026): the save lives behind the **SaveStore** (`src/platform/storage/SaveStore.ts`; same keys, 2 rotating backups, plus `the_swarm_save_unreadable`); nothing outside platform/ touches `localStorage`. Loading gives a **load status**: `ok`, `recoveredFromBackup` (no primary; the backup loads, with a warning and one toast), `unreadable` (bad JSON, a newer version or a failing migration) or `empty`. An unreadable save is **never silently reset**: its text is kept aside, every save is held, and `THIS SAVE COULDN'T BE READ.` offers LOAD BACKUP and START OVER. While writes fail, `• SAVE FAILED` sits under the strip and offers EXPORT SAVE; the last good save is untouched.
Story 2.12 (ADR 0030): **durability** (`platform.durability`, `src/platform/storage/durability.ts`). At the **first meaningful play** (the first committed phase, or a load already past Egg Laying) the game asks `navigator.storage.persist()` once ever (the ask is remembered under `the_swarm_storage_persist`) and logs the outcome under `storage`; on a mobile browser (`capabilities.mobile`, a coarse pointer) that isn't the installed app it shows `ADD THE GAME TO YOUR HOME SCREEN.` once (`the_swarm_home_hint`). Both keys live outside the save.

### Offline install (PWA)

Story 2.13 (ADR 0031): the production build is an installable PWA. vite-plugin-pwa (build-time only, `vite.config.ts`) writes `manifest.webmanifest` (standalone, portrait, scoped to `/the-swarm/`, icons in `public/icons/`) and `sw.js`, which **precaches every emitted file** (`tests/unit/build/pwaBuild.test.ts` fails on any file left out). main registers it through `platform.serviceWorker` (`src/platform/serviceWorker.ts`) in production builds only; dev and E2E builds register only with `?sw=1` (`tests/e2e/offline-pwa.spec.ts`). **Update rule:** a new version installs and *waits* for the next cold start (no `skipWaiting`, no `clientsClaim`, never a reload); the player sees `A NEW VERSION IS READY. IT LOADS THE NEXT TIME THE GAME STARTS.` once per session. A return to the app checks for a new version.

### Notifications
Story 2.14 (ADR 0032): **no notifications in the web build** (owner). `platform.notifications` (`src/platform/notifications.ts`) is a seam for a store build: `schedule({id, atMs, title, body})`, `cancel(id)`, `cancelAll()`, `available`. The web implementation is a no-op (`available: false`); nothing asks for notification permission, nothing shows one, and settings have no notifications toggle. Only main holds the service (it cancels pending reminders on return); no game layer, ui/ or actions/ receives it, and nothing in `src/` names a browser notification API (`tests/arch/architecture.test.ts`).

### Swarm Mono and type roles
The game's own monospace font (a JetBrains Mono NL subset, `public/fonts/`) and the six type roles
(`--type-display/title/value/body/aside/meta`, rem) in `src/ui/styles/typography.css`. Every size is a role;
sentence case is never below `aside`; text glow only from `value` up. ADR 0041.

### Sound
Story 8.8 (ADR 0044): **sound is off by default** and plays only through `platform.audio` (`src/platform/audio/`). It is synthesized at play time with Web Audio, and the game has no audio files. It makes no audio context before the first user gesture with sound on, and it is silent where Web Audio is missing. The cues: `key` (a tap: lay egg or a buy, at most 8/s), `hatch` (at most 1/s), `victory`/`defeat`/`draw`, `phase`, `decision` (a two-tone chime), `prestige`, `discovery`. `SOUND_EVENTS` (`platform/feedback.ts`) pairs every sounding event with the UI hook that shows the same moment. Audio is never the only channel.

### Keyboard shortcuts

Story 1.30 (ADR 0021): `ui/shell/shortcuts.ts` acts on the player's key bindings (settings, Story 1.27). The **primary** key (Space) presses the active tab's primary-zone button as a tap would, only with focus on the page or that button; tab keys (1–5) press the tab-bar buttons; **pause** (P) pauses (any key, P included, resumes through the PAUSED overlay); **close** (Esc) dismisses a non-decision sheet through `SheetController.dismiss()`. Off in settings means no shortcut fires, Esc included. Hover tips show on tap too and Esc dismisses them (`ui/shell/tips.ts`).

### Decision events
A **decision event** (Story 1.28, ADR 0019) is a two-choice situation from `src/data/content/decisions.ts`, with an **ignore policy**: `free` (ignoring costs nothing), `costly` (ignoring applies `ignore`; a timeout is logged and toasted) or `unavoidable` (no timer, must be answered). At most one is open, in `state.decision.open` (saved, with `remainingMs`). Events spawn on the **session clock** (foreground play since this launch, ≥ 2 min), never during an overlay or the offline summary, and never offline. The countdown runs on game time only while the decision sheet is on screen; the decision-timer setting doubles or removes it. Choices and timeouts go through `applyChoice` (`src/systems/DecisionSystem.ts`).

Story 4.5 (FR13, FR14): each MVP phase has **its own events**, at least three unique to it (Egg Laying: COLD SNAP., A FALLEN SEED., FLOODED CHAMBER.; Colony: BEETLE SIGHTING. (costly), OVERCROWDING. (unavoidable), APHIDS ON THE STEM.; Combat: THE WOUNDED RETURN., SHELLS ON THE FIELD., AN ENVOY ARRIVES.); SCOUT REPORT. stays the Expansion and Space event. Effects name a **decision stock** (`DECISION_STOCKS`: food, workers, capacity, eggs and larvae with their pipelines, wood, stone, nectar, guards, combat soldiers, chitin, scouts), each with the first phase it exists in, so an event can't name or take a stock early (the catch-22 test covers events too). A worker loss takes free workers first, then workers off their roles (guard last), never researchers, and **never wipes the colony**: one outcome leaves at least `decisionBalance.workerFloorShare` of the workers (rounded up, so never the last one). An event can wait for a state (`spawnsWhen: 'crowded'`: OVERCROWDING. needs the nest `crowdedShare` full). An unavoidable event can't be ignored (`applyChoice(…, 'ignore')` changes nothing) and always has a choice with no requirement. Each choice's cost line shows every amount it changes and its odds (`1 IN N`), checked by `tests/unit/data/decisions.test.ts`; `tests/unit/systems/decisionOutcomes.test.ts` is the property test.

### Next action
The one most useful thing to do now (Story 1.21, UX-DR12, ADR 0015): pure `getNextAction(state)` in `src/systems/NextAction.ts`, read by the UI as `selectNextAction`. First match wins: goal latched → CONTINUE; food critical (net rate < 0 and the stock covers less than `nextActionBalance.foodCriticalMs` of upkeep) → a worker to GATHER (or freed from TEND, FORAGE, DIG, or a spare GUARD first); capacity critical (workers ≥ `capacityCriticalPercent` of the effective capacity) → Warehouse if affordable, else a worker to DIG; an affordable item from the phase's **critical-path table** (`src/data/content/criticalPath.ts`); any affordable item; otherwise none, with `NEXT AFFORDABLE: <item> IN ~<time>`. Items are only suggested from panels open in the phase, and every suggestion is an `ActionIntent` that `actions.perform(intent)` accepts. `ui/shell/NextActionButton` keeps the target under the thumb while held and for `targetLockMs` after the last touch.

**BUILD's highlighted card** (Story 1.24): `getBuildAction(state)` (same file, `selectBuildAction`) runs the item rules over the items BUILD sells only (`buildItems`: Click Power from Phase 2, the buildings, the research chain): an affordable critical-path item, then any affordable one, else none with the BUILD item that becomes affordable soonest. That card is drawn `data-selected`, BUILD's pinned `> BUY` buys it, and with none the BUILD header reads `NEXT: <item> IN ~<time>`. Click Power is sold by NEST in Phase 1 and by BUILD (`build_click_power`) after it. The BUILD UI is `src/ui/build/` (placed by `ui/gameViews.tsx`, ADR 0025).

**Egg-chamber upgrades** (Story 4.6, FR19): Phase 1 sells WARM BROOD (hatch rate), LEAN RATIONS (worker upkeep) and SIDE CHAMBER (nest rooms, added to `resources.nestCapacity` when bought) beside Click Power, all for food, as NEST cards (`src/ui/nest/NestUpgradeCard.tsx`). Numbers and per-level effects are data in `src/data/balance/upgrades.ts` (`upgradeBalance`, `NEST_UPGRADE_IDS`), copy in `src/data/content/upgrades.ts`. They are sold only in Egg Laying, up to a last level (`isUpgradeOpen`/`upgradeRefusal` in ResourceSystem); their levels keep working afterwards. `itemEffects(ref)` in NextAction gives an item's effects as data, so `tests/unit/data/phase1Choices.test.ts` checks that no Phase 1 item is strictly dominant. Click Power stays the only tap upgrade.

### Chronicle (event log)
The colony's event lines (Story 1.26, ADR 0018): `src/state/chronicle.ts`, newest first, last 100, saved with the game in `SaveData.chronicle` beside the game state (not in it: lines are written by event listeners). One-time lines are chronicle **milestones**, so a reload never repeats them; a prestige starts a new chronicle. NEST shows the newest 3; LOG (`src/ui/log/`) shows all of them, then lifetime stats, the settings entry, and **LEGACY** (prestige and the Legacy tree) once the prestige unlock condition is met (`selectLegacyUnlocked`). A line with a `target` panel jumps to it when tapped.

### Pipelines
Rate-based O(1) processing: `eggPipeline`, `larvaPipeline`, `soldierPipeline`. Each has `count` (rate) and `progress` (accumulated). No per-item timers. Story 2.5 (ADR 0028): a step moves the exact share `1 − e^(−k·dt)` of a pipeline (`src/utils/decay.ts`), plus the eggs that hatch and mature within the same step, so one long step moves what many short ones do. Every egg laid (tap, automation, the prestige auto egg layer, the royal cache) is in `eggPipeline.count`: `resources.eggs === eggPipeline.count` and `resources.larvae === larvaPipeline.count`.

### Prestige
Reset loop: sacrifice progress for Legacy Points (LP). A prestige awards `legacyPointsAward(state)`: lifetime food produced and the phase reached (`calculateLegacyPoints()` in `PrestigeSystem.ts`) plus an entropy bonus, constants in `data/balance/prestige.ts` (`prestigeBalance.lp`). The award goes to the LP held (`prestige.legacyPoints`) and to **lifetime LP** (`prestige.lifetimeLpEarned`, Story 5.2, FR29). The production bonus (`productionMultiplierFor(state)` in `ProgressionCurve.ts`, +15 % per LP) counts lifetime LP, so spending LP on a permanent upgrade in the prestige tree (`src/data/prestigeTree.ts`) never lowers it; lifetime LP always equals the LP held plus the LP spent. Requires all buildings Lv.5 + 100K food lifetime. **Prestige wipes the run** (Story 5.3, FR30, ADR 0040): the new state is `createInitialState()` plus the fields in `PRESTIGE_KEEPS` (`seed`, `rng`, `prestige`, `prestigeTree`, `stats`, `lifetime`, `victoryAchieved`, `automation`, `nextIds`, `lastSaveTimestamp`) and the kills tally (`soldiers.totalKilled`). Every resource (void crystals, antimatter, dark matter and survey data too), building, research project, mutation, piece of equipment, battle count, territory, expedition, ship, exploration, the goal latch and any open decision event start over. Owned Legacy upgrades grant the royal cache's eggs and food and Phase Skip's start in Colony. The next enemy is the kept generator's first draw for a run with no battles won. The log opens the new chronicle with `A QUEEN, AGAIN.`; settings live outside the save and are untouched. Save v32 (Story 5.4): a save from before that rule which has prestiged and whose run is before Space loses the space resources it carried (they go to 0); a run in Space or Transcendence, or a save that never prestiged, keeps them. **The victory record** (Story 5.5, FR31): `victoryAchieved` is written once, by the Transcendence transition, kept by prestige and saved; only `state/selectors.ts`, `ui/` and the save read it (`tests/arch/victoryRecord.test.ts`), so it changes nothing in play (`tests/unit/victoryRecord.test.ts`: two colonies differing only in the record stay identical through random play). LOG keeps LEGACY after the ending (`selectLegacyUnlocked`) and shows the victory as a permanent lifetime row (`[data-lifetime-stat="victory"]`). LOG's BATTLES WON is the lifetime tally `stats.totalBattlesWon` (save v33), since `battlesWon` is this run's count. **Phase Skip** (Story 5.6, FR32): `lifetime.phasesCompleted` (save v34) lists every phase a run has moved out of; `PhaseStateMachine.commit` adds it and prestige keeps it. `startPhaseFor(state)` (`PrestigeSystem.ts`) starts the next run in Egg Laying, or with Phase Skip in Colony, but never later than the first phase not completed. The v33 → v34 step derives the list from what the save shows (the phases before the current one and before each seen reveal; a prestige implies Egg Laying and Colony; the victory, every phase before Transcendence). The random-play driver shared by the property tests is `tests/unit/helpers/randomPlay.ts`; new purchasables and verbs go in its `arbAction`.

### Combat Resources
Dropped by enemies in the COMBAT phase. Used for crafting, equipment upgrades, and colony mutations.
- **Silk**: spider drop. Used for armor (soldier defense) and Silk Weaving building.
- **Chitin**: beetle/scorpion drop. Used for weapon (soldier strength) and Chitin Armory building.
- **Venom**: scorpion drop. Used for poison damage (combat DoT) and Venom Lab building.

### Battle and the enemy card
A **battle** is one `actions.fight()`: it fights the **next enemy** (`state.nextEnemy`, saved; Story 4.1, ADR 0037), resolves at once (one write) and ends in **victory**, **defeat** or **draw** (the enemy flees after `MAX_BATTLE_ROUNDS`); `BattleResult` carries the `outcome` and the `rounds` fought, and the battle draws the next enemy from the seeded generator. Each enemy type has one **trait** (`data/content/enemies.ts`: swarm, regrowth, web, thick shell, swift, venom resistance) that changes the stats it names, so different armies suit different enemies. The **enemy card** (FRONT → WAR, Story 1.25, ADR 0017) shows the next enemy with its trait (`ARMORED BEETLE · THICK SHELL`) and the trait's line, and a matchup hint that plays the rounds once with fixed rolls; the **round counter** is a short replay of the rounds after the battle is resolved, then the **battle summary** sheet opens at `battle` priority. Training a combat soldier takes a free worker. **An army has a real cost** (Story 4.4, FR12): each soldier takes `combatBalance.army.workersPerSoldier` workers to train and eats `combatBalance.army.upkeepFoodPerSec` while it lives, trainees included (`armySize` = `combatSoldiers + soldierPipeline.count`, which training never changes, so the upkeep is exact for any step and in catch-up's closed form). The upkeep is part of `computeResourceRates().foodConsumedPerSec`, so it moves net food, food-critical, starving and the offline summary. WAR shows `SOLDIERS n · UPKEEP x FOOD/S` (`[data-army-upkeep]`, `selectArmyUpkeepPerSec`) and TRAIN's cost line names the upkeep. A **starving** colony (`isStarving`: an empty store and more eaten than made, the same rule as `selectStarving`) trains no soldiers: `trainSoldier` refuses `starving` (`THE COLONY IS STARVING`). Every weapon and armor level and every mutation is a **counter**: strong against some traits and weak (no or a weaker bonus) against others (`combatBalance.equipment` / `combatBalance.mutations`, Story 4.2, ADR 0038), checked against the real fight by `tests/sim/counters.test.ts`; the cards say `BEST AGAINST <TRAIT>`. A **battle cause** (`BattleCause`: a kind plus the enemy's trait) is answered by `counterFor(cause, state)` (`src/systems/Counters.ts`), the cheapest open card strong against that trait. Every battle carries its cause (Story 4.3, `BattleResult.cause`, emitted with `battle_completed`), decided by `battleCause()` in `src/systems/BattleCauses.ts` from the army and the trait, never from a roll (a loss: `outnumbered`, `trait_beat_mutation`, `trait_beat_equipment`, `equipment_gap`; a victory: `trait_countered`, `outfought`; plus the `upgrade` the line names). The **battle summary** shows the headline (VICTORY, DEFEAT, `DRAW · THEY RETREATED.`), losses, drops (only after a victory), one **cause line** (`causeLine()`, words in `data/content/battleCauses.ts`: `THEIR SHELLS TURNED YOUR VENOM.`) and, after a defeat or draw, `> CHANGE → <card>`: `battleAdvice(cause, state)` gives `counterFor`'s card, its shortfall, and a cheaper counter the colony can pay for now; CHANGE jumps to the card on WAR (`LineJump.jump(panel, within)`).

### Equipment
Soldier upgrades purchased with combat resources. `weapon` (chitin) → strength, `armor` (silk) → defense + HP. Upgraded via dedicated buildings.

### Colony Mutations
One-time permanent bonuses purchased with combat resources. Stacking upgrades (e.g. +10% gather, +1 soldier speed, faster larvae hatching).

### Expeditions
Send scouts to map tiles (EXPANSION phase) to harvest resources. Tiles yield: Forest → wood + silk, Mountain → stone + chitin, Meadow → food + venom. Enemy Nests require combat first, then loot.

### Resources
Integers only (`Math.floor()`). Food, wood, stone, nectar (phases 1-4). Void crystals, antimatter, dark matter (phase 5+). Rate-credited amounts go through `creditWhole` (`src/utils/math.ts`): the whole part is credited, the fraction carries in **`resourceRemainder`** (save v21) for food, wood, stone and nectar, and in `conversions.remainder` for each conversion's next run.

Story 3.3 (FR34, ADR 0033): **every count and every cost is a whole number.** `tests/unit/engine/integerResources.test.ts` drives random seeded states through random ticks, actions and catch-ups and checks after every step that each `state.resources` value (and combat loot, soldiers) is an integer ≥ 0. `tests/unit/wholeCosts.test.ts` scans every cost in `data/balance/` (any value under a key naming a cost, growth factors aside) and the derived level costs. Building effects that count things (`nestCapacity`, `scoutsCap`, `warriorsCap`) are floored after the soft cap. Dark Matter Detection costs 1 antimatter (was 0.1). **Entropy** is a 0–100 meter, not a resource: `state.entropy`, outside `state.resources`, may hold a fraction, is shown rounded (`entropyMeter`, `selectEntropyMeter`) and is never spent; only prestige resets it. Save v25 floors any fractional count an older save holds.

### Glyphs

Story 8.5 (FR44, UX-DR34, ADR 0045): the **nest scene** (`src/ui/scenes/NestScene.tsx`) draws the colony as a cross-section: soil, round-capped tunnels, a **brood chamber** (the queen, eggs, larvae) and a **worker chamber**. ### Step length
Story 2.5 (ADR 0028): `processTick(state, …, events, dtSec)` (`src/engine/processTick.ts`) is the one simulation step: main's live tick (50 ms, `TICK_MS` in `src/data/constants.ts`) runs it, then adds the foreground-only parts (decision events, the latch); offline catch-up runs it too. Every system in it gives the same result for one step of `n × 50 ms` as for `n` steps of 50 ms, within the tolerance stated in `tests/unit/engine/stepLength.test.ts`. The Phase 4–6 values still in ticks (mission length) are converted with `TICK_MS` until their epics move them.

Story 3.2 (FR40, AR19/AR20): **balance lives in data files.** Every cost, rate, timer and cap of Phases 1–3 is in `src/data/balance/<system>.ts`, one `as const` object per system (`resources`, `soldiers`, `buildings`, `progression`, `enemies`, `combat`, `mutations`, `automation`, `research`, `recruitment`, `prestigeBonuses`, `decisions`, `nextAction`); structural numbers (`MS_PER_SEC`, `PERCENT`, `TICK_MS`) are in `src/data/constants.ts`. Durations are `…Ms`, rates `…PerSec`; nothing is in ticks. The old "tick" constants were already per second in the pipelines (hatch, mature, train: 50 s, 50 s, 75 s), so they became 50 000 / 50 000 / 75 000 ms and pacing is unchanged. `tests/arch/balanceLiterals.test.ts` fails on any numeric literal other than 0 and 1 in `systems/` (and the balance engine files), except the shrink-only `PENDING_BALANCE_LITERALS` list (the Phase 4–6 and Legacy systems), and on balance keys that name a tick or lack a unit suffix. Tests import balance numbers from `data/balance/`; phase-guard targets stay in `transitions.ts`.

### Simulated player
Story 3.7 (AR15, ADR 0034): `src/sim/` plays the real game headless. `runSim({ state, seed, schedule, policy?, stopWhen? })` (or `new SimRunner(...)`) moves time only through `resume(elapsedMs)` (each **check-in**'s gap in one call, the check-in itself in `simBalance.activeStepMs` looks) and makes moves only through `actions/`, on its own bus and store. The **check-in schedule** is a list of `{ awayMs, activeMs }` (`dailySchedule(days)`: `simBalance.checkInsPerDay` a day with the overnight gap). The **default policy** follows the next action, rebalances roles, taps LAY EGG in Egg Laying, puts a worker on a role the goal counts (GUARD), and trains and fights in Combat; decision events never fire. The **probes** give each phase entry (real and played ms) and, per check-in, actions by name, taps, refusals and the decisions available.

Story 3.9 (AR15 report, FR40, ADR 0034): **`pnpm sim`** (`scripts/sim.mjs`, not CI; `--seed N --days N`) plays a fresh game over `dailySchedule(days)` and prints the **pacing report** (`src/sim/report.ts`: `runPacingReport`, `buildPacingReport`, `formatPacingReport`): per MVP phase the active-play and real-time duration next to the GDD targets with a FAST/ok/SLOW verdict, the later phases reached, the share of eggs from tapping in the first 2 min and from Colony on, the median actions per check-in, and every **idle check-in** (a pacing defect: on arrival nothing could be done within `idleCheckInMaxMs`, about 4 h, and no move landed during it). The targets are `pacingTargets` in `src/data/balance/pacing.ts`; tests never assert them. The probes also ledger every egg laid (`eggs`: laid, tapped, phase) and, per check-in, `waitMs` (0 when a move is open on arrival, else the wait for the soonest affordable item, null when never).

Story 3.11 (FR40, ADR 0036): **Phases 1–3 are paced by the brood pipeline and the nest's room.** Phase 1 waits on a ~15 min hatch and ~15 min mature; Colony and Combat wait on nest capacity (slow digging, the Warehouse as the big step), with wood, stone and nectar yields and building prices set per offline gap. The sim player taps while its goal lacks workers (always in Egg Laying, later only while the waiting brood doesn't cover the shortfall). Probes flag `foodCritical` / `capacityCritical` per check-in; the report counts them for `pacingTargets.criticalMoments`. Story 4.4: the **max-army policy** (`maxArmyPolicy` in `src/sim/policy.ts`: in Combat it trains every free worker it can pay for before any other move, fights once nothing more can be trained, and moves a gatherer back to DIG when the nest is full and food allows) plays Combat against the default policy from the same entry state, seed and schedule (`runArmyComparison`, `compareArmies`); the report's army trade-off section lists the check-ins where only the max-army colony is food-critical. `tests/sim/reachability.test.ts` runs every MVP phase for both policies. Before/after reports: `_bmad-output/implementation-artifacts/3-11-tune-phases-1-3.md`.

### Play telemetry
Story 3.10 (FR38, NFR10, ADR 0035): a **telemetry** ring buffer (`src/state/telemetry.ts`) kept beside the game under its own key (`platform.telemetry`, `the_swarm_telemetry`), **outside the save**. A **check-in** is one foreground session, from arrival (cold start or return to the page) to the page hiding; each records its length, the time away before it, the phase and the **decisions available** on arrival (`decisionsAvailable` in `systems/NextAction.ts`: affordable items plus CONTINUE when latched, the sim's count too). Each phase left records its foreground (`activeMs`) and wall (`elapsedMs`) time. At most `TELEMETRY_MAX_ENTRIES` entries, oldest dropped first. It leaves the device only as the file settings' `EXPORT TELEMETRY` downloads; the game makes no network request (`tests/arch/noNetwork.test.ts`, `tests/e2e/telemetry.spec.ts`).

### Automation targets
Story 4.10 (FR16, UX-DR16, ADR 0039): an **automatable verb** is one the player does by hand: laying (`lay`), soldier training (`train`), recruiting scouts and warriors (`scout`, `warrior`), listed in `src/data/content/automation.ts`. Its **automation target row** (`KEEP [−] N [+] IN TRAINING [AUTO]`, `src/ui/shell/AutomationRow.tsx`) appears under the verb after `automationBalance.targets.unlockAfterManual` manual performances (counted in `gameState.automation.performed`, kept by prestige) or from the phase after the verb's own. With AUTO on, `tickAutomationTargets` (`src/systems/AutomationTargets.ts`, inside `processTick`, so offline too) works the **count** toward the **target** and never past it: the brood (eggs and larvae), the army (trained and training), scouts and warriors (home and away). It pays what the manual verb pays, says `holding at N` or why it can't (`food short`, `no free workers`, `barracks full`), and never touches a decision event. The queen lays on her own at `layTapsPerSec` taps a second, far slower than tapping. Save v30.
